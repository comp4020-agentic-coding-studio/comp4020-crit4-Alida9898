# Crit 4 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Instruments and music are not a subject I know or care much about, and I went
into this deliverable with little personal insight into the topic. What did
move the work forward was realising this was less a music project than an
exercise in directing Claude well. Applying the workflow habits from this
course — writing an explicit plan before building, pinning gestures and rules
to tests rather than trusting a look, recording decisions and their reasons in
CLAUDE.md — kept Claude's output aligned with what I actually wanted, and,
just as usefully, sharpened how I drew ideas out of the conversation itself. I
came to treat this project less as "build a water-glass instrument" and more
as a small trial of a working method: can a workflow make an agent produce
something coherent on a topic I have no natural feel for?

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It confirmed that I want to be a developer who leads with my own judgement
rather than being led by the model's. Because I was not invested in the
subject, I initially asked Claude to generate direction for me, and what it
proposed was consistently not what I would have chosen; it read as generic
rather than considered. That experience left me with a conclusion I want to
carry forward: an AI cannot make the decision for you, but it is an excellent
executor, and even in the process of talking things through with it, it can
genuinely inspire you. So the right response is not to resist that process
but to enjoy it — steering it with my own judgement while staying open to
what it turns up along the way.
